FRIDGI

I treated fruit ripeness like a signal-processing problem.

Fridgi is a smart food-storage system that tracks changes inside a container, alerts users when fruit reaches ideal ripeness, and can actively filter aging gases when consumption is delayed.

PROBLEM → PRODUCT / 01

Fruit changes before the outside tells you enough.

The project focused on a simple household problem: ripeness is difficult to judge continuously from appearance alone. Fridgi turns changes inside the container into a signal users can act on instead of relying only on memory or guesswork.

SYSTEM / 02

One container.
Three coordinated layers.

The concept combines sensing, software interpretation, and active air treatment rather than treating storage as a passive plastic box.

01 SOFTWARE

Track the rate of change.

The interface uses the selected fruit type to interpret gas-change trends over time and sends a phone notification when the data reaches the target ripeness range.

BananaJUST RIPE
02 HARDWARE

Sense the environment.

The lid integrates an ESP32, calibrated gas sensing, humidity measurement, and a micro-fan above a clear Tritan container body.

ESP32GASRHFAN
03 ACTIVE INTERVENTION

React when eating is delayed.

The design routes air through a potassium-permanganate filter pad to remove aging gases, with a target shelf-life extension of roughly five to seven days.

ENGINEERING TRADE-OFF / 03

Use software to make affordable sensors more useful.

Instead of competing with industrial gas-analysis hardware on raw sensor cost, the concept relies on algorithmic filtering to account for environmental noise and changes in fruit volume.

BUSINESS MODEL / 04

The prototype had to make financial sense, too.

Fridgi was designed as both a consumer smart-hardware product and a B2B supermarket system, combining upfront hardware sales with recurring filter and software revenue.

The market plan moves from a controlled household/retail pilot to subscriptions, digital distribution, enterprise logistics, and eventually appliance integration.

SWOT / 06

What could make it work — and what could break it.

The deck also forced the team to think beyond the prototype: recurring revenue, usability friction, market growth, and copycat risk all affect whether the product survives outside a classroom.

STRENGTH

Hardware + recurring revenue

Hardware margin, replacement filters, B2B subscriptions, and software that lowers physical production cost.

WEAKNESS

Manual fruit selection

The current mobile experience requires users to choose the fruit type before tracking begins.

OPPORTUNITY

Smart-home + waste reduction

The project targets demand for automation and technologies that reduce waste across households and retail.

THREAT

Copycat hardware

The generic container is easy to imitate, so the deck positions the data-filtering logic as the more defensible layer.

ROLE + OUTCOME / 07

I had to think like both an engineer and a product builder.

My work spanned the technical blueprint and BOM, sensing and monitoring logic, active-filtration concept, pricing structure, and B2C/B2B business model.
