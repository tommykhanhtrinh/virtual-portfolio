A better starting point

This season began with more clarity.

High Stakes taught me how much a robot depends on the decisions made before the first part is assembled. In Push Back, I came in with a stronger sense of structure: a clearer drivetrain plan, more purposeful mechanism choices, and a deeper habit of learning through iteration rather than improvisation alone.

01

Start with the frame.

I paid more attention to the robot’s foundation: drive layout, weight balance, structure, and enough room for later mechanisms.

02

Build with the game in mind.

Instead of just making mechanisms move, every subsystem had to serve a match purpose: scoring, positioning, and repeatability under pressure.

03

Scrimmage as a real test.

SSIS Scrimmage was not just a warm-up. It was the first proof that our build, preparation, and team coordination could work in a real competition setting.

04

One chapter in a bigger run.

This page now captures the first two connected phases of the season: the scrimmage breakthrough and the rebuilt robot prepared for Southern Regional.

Between events · rebuild

After scrimmage, we rebuilt the robot for Southern Regional.

SSIS Scrimmage gave us momentum, but it also made the next decision clear: do not stay comfortable with the first version. Before Southern Regional, we rebuilt the robot with a more deliberate architecture so the next chapter would not simply repeat the scrimmage machine — it would improve on it.

Post-scrimmage reset

Keep the lessons.
Rebuild the machine.

The rebuild was the real bridge between chapter 01 and chapter 02. Instead of treating the scrimmage result as the finish line, we treated it as feedback. The robot was rethought from the drivebase upward, mechanisms were reorganized, and the whole build direction became more intentional for Southern Regional.

01Drivebase revisit

Return to the fundamentals before stacking more complexity on top.

02Structure redesign

Refine how the frame and scoring system interact as one machine.

03Regional focus

Build toward a stronger, more stable robot for the next competition chapter.

1
Prototype firstWe revisited basic structures and early mechanisms so the next version could start from stronger foundations.
2
Use CAD as a bridgeThe rebuild was not only physical. CAD helped connect ideas, proportions, and mechanism direction before the robot was fully rebuilt.
3
Test with purposeBy the time Southern Regional approached, the rebuild had shifted from experimentation to targeted preparation.
Rebuild takeawayThe important part of this chapter is not only that a new robot appeared. It is that the team was willing to iterate boldly between events — a habit that matters in every serious VEX season.
