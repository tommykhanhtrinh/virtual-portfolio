CHAPTER 02 · 2025 · VICE CAMP LEADER

Career Summer CampI returned to the same place — but not in the same role.

In 2024, my world was the lesson in front of me. In 2025, I had to widen the frame: people, schedules, classrooms, equipment, teaching teams, cultural exchange, and the atmosphere that held all of it together.

ACCESS→COORDINATION→TRUST→OWNERSHIP
See the role shift ↓

The place was familiar. The responsibility was not.

The biggest change was not a new lesson plan. It was the question I carried into the camp. In 2024 I asked how to explain one idea better. In 2025 I had to ask how the entire camp could work better.

2024 · Teaching-team member

How do I explain this better?

I focused on hesitation, clarity, pacing, and confidence one learner at a time.

→
2025 · Vice Camp Leader

How do we make the whole camp work?

My responsibility expanded to the conditions around every lesson: access, coordination, energy, people, and trust.

Service started before anyone sat down to learn.

We moved and set up desktop computers, checked rooms, prepared materials, and handled the work no one photographs when it goes smoothly. That changed my idea of access: sometimes it begins with carrying a machine across a room and making sure it turns on.

Leadership became less about standing at the front and more about making the invisible parts dependable.

One camp. Many classrooms.

Career Summer Camp moved between English, Mandarin, MOS and presentation skills, career guidance, STEM making, and life skills. My job was no longer to own one subject, but to help different teaching teams create practical, inviting experiences.

Language · confidence first

Make speaking feel safer than staying silent.

English and Mandarin sessions worked best when students could laugh, gesture, answer imperfectly, and still feel that they belonged in the conversation.

Digital access · from device to agency

A computer only becomes access when someone knows what to do with it.

MOS and presentation lessons connected the donated hardware to confidence: not simply putting a machine in front of a student, but helping them create something they could call their own.

Career guidance · make the future touchable

“Career” became clearer when students could build, draw, test, and imagine.

Simple STEM vehicles, a bamboo stall, creative tasks, and conversations about students' own dreams made the future less abstract.

Then the classroom opened into the community.

Cultural exchange, bamboo dancing, field work, meals, and shared chores changed the direction of learning. We were no longer only the people bringing activities. We were guests learning local rhythms and doing unfamiliar things badly before doing them a little better.

The most honest version of service was exchange: everyone had something to share and something to learn.

By the last days, the schedule had turned into people I did not want to leave.

Closing activities, certificates, photos, gifts, and the bus ride away made the emotional side of the camp impossible to ignore. Students who began as names on a list had become personalities I could recognize across the room.

Kon Tum did not end when we left Kon Tum.

Back at Đinh Thiện Lý, we brought part of the exchange onto the school stage. With the Taiwanese university team, the relationship also continued through cultural visits such as Biển Hồ (Hồ T'Nưng). The camp had an ending date; the exchange did not.

What changed between 2024 and 2025: in 2024, service taught me how to make one explanation clearer. In 2025, leadership taught me to widen the frame — to think about the infrastructure before the lesson, the team behind it, the student inside it, and the relationships that remain afterward. I returned to the same place, but I did not return as the same person.
